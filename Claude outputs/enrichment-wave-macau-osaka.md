# MACAU (2)

## two-moons-macau
name: Two Moons
city: Macau
country: Macau
address: 32A Rua de Pedro Nolasco da Silva, Macau
neighborhood: Historic Centre
description: Two Moons sits on Rua de Pedro Nolasco da Silva in the historic centre of Macau, directly across from the Consulate General of Portugal, and was opened in 2020 by Frankie Leong, who had tended bar at Milroy's of Soho, the oldest independent whisky specialist in London. It began as a haven for whisky drinkers and grew into a bar devoted to fine spirits more broadly, with shelves that reach well past Scotland. By day the room works as a coffee shop, and the coffee is treated with the same care as the drinks poured later. The cocktail list is deliberately short, numbered simply one to ten, and ranges from Spanish brandy to Japanese shochu; an eleventh option is whatever the bar team builds around a guest's taste.

## wood-house-macau
name: Wood House
city: Macau
country: Macau
address: UG/F, Unit A, Edificio Cheong Seng, 30 Rua Central, Macau
neighborhood: Historic Centre
description: Wood House hides in plain sight on Rua Central, around the corner from St Augustine's Square, and opened during the pandemic in 2021. It is an intimate local haunt rather than a destination bar, and it fills up: on a busy weekend the ground floor runs out of room and guests carry on up to the second. On a quieter night a guest can take a seat at the narrow first-floor bar and work through a list of roughly twenty signature drinks, which runs from the fruity and easy to stiffer builds drawn from the classics. The bartenders are happy to talk it through and work out a flavour profile rather than hand over a menu.

# OSAKA, JAPAN (1)

## ista-coffee-elements
name: Ista Coffee Elements
city: Osaka
country: Japan
neighborhood: Sakaisuji-Hommachi
description: Ista Coffee Elements is an eight-seat room near Sakaisuji-Hommachi station in the business district of central Osaka, where everything turns on coffee. Owner and bartender Fumiaki Nozato is a world coffee cocktail champion, and the beans are roasted in house on a micro-roaster tucked away at the end of the narrow bar before they become the base of the drinks. It works as a coffee shop pulling espresso and as a cocktail bar with one of the most cohesive lists in the city. The Maillard Banana Shake builds on banana, rum, milk, chocolate and espresso; the Element, a competition winner, sets coffee against brandy, rose, raspberry, cream and Campari. A section called Coffee Tree Cocktails reaches past the bean to the cherries, the flowers and the leaves.
